# Regulatory watch — cross-border crypto, South Africa

> **Status: verified** for the technique and the sources below (retrieved 2026-10-08).
> **Standing caveat: the cross-border framework is DRAFT.** It has no statutory force. It is read
> as having the effect of law by the Financial Surveillance Department once in force, so design
> assumptions should track it — but no document in this repo may describe it as law.

---

## 1. The two draft instruments

| Instrument | Status | Dates | Authority |
|---|---|---|---|
| Draft Crypto Asset Manual for cross-border activities | **DRAFT** — comment closed | Published 2026-08-03; comments closed 2026-09-30 | National Treasury + SARB |
| Draft Capital Flow Management Regulations, 2026 | **DRAFT** — comment open | Published 2026-04-17 | Government Notice 54520, Gazette 7375 |

Read together. The Manual gives practical effect to the Regulations. A closed comment window is
**not** an enactment date — check the Government Gazette before treating either as in force.

## 2. The rules that matter

**Trigger point.** A transaction becomes cross-border when crypto assets move between a domestic
Authorised CASP and an offshore CASP, or from a domestic Authorised CASP to a non-custodial
wallet, producing an inflow or outflow reportable to FinSurv.

**The entity restriction — read this before quoting any B2B corridor.** At this stage **only
individuals** may externalise crypto assets via Authorised CASPs, within the single discretionary
allowance and the foreign capital allowance. **Resident entities are prohibited** from
cross-border crypto transactions classified as import or export of capital.

That single line decides whether the agricultural trade corridors can settle through the rail as
currently structured. For a B2B goods business it is the most consequential rule in the framework.

**Domestic by default.** Transfers between domestic Authorised CASPs are deemed domestic and
non-reportable.

**No FX dealing.** Authorised CASPs are prohibited from dealing in foreign currency, and
settlement between a South African client and an Authorised CASP takes place in Rand.

**Three categories.** Category One — remittance between individuals using crypto as a settlement
medium. Category Two — specific cross-border crypto asset transactions. Category Three — a
combination.

## 3. The licensing stack

Three parallel regimes, with overlapping obligations:

| Regime | Regulator | Instrument |
|---|---|---|
| Conduct / licensing | FSCA | FAIS Act 37 of 2002 |
| AML / CFT / CFP | FSCA supervises, FIC registers | FIC Act 38 of 2001 |
| Capital flow | FinSurv, SARB | Exchange Control Regulations + draft Manual |

**Sequence matters:** FSCA licence → FIC registration → FinSurv application → conditional approval
→ six-month compliance window. No grace period and **no published processing timeframe**, so a
go-live date cannot currently be planned from the rules alone.

FSCA position as at 2026-03-31: **533** CASP licence applications received, **310** approved,
**17** declined, **124** withdrawn, leaving **82** under review.

Note the overlap: FIC CDD obligations and the Manual's parallel requirements cover much of the
same ground (KYC, record-keeping, source-of-funds). Where they overlap, evidence should be
collected once and mapped to both — not twice.

## 4. What is not published

Say "not published", never estimate:

- application fee — none is payable
- processing timeframe — none published
- transitional provisions for CASPs already registered and licensed
- treatment of transactions entered into before commencement

These gaps are the substance of the industry's consultation response, and they are why the
sequential path can leave operators unable to offer cross-border services for a long period.

## 5. Legal status of crypto assets

Crypto assets are **not legal tender** in South Africa, and the SARB does not recognise them as
currency. The FSCA's licensing powers cover advice, intermediary and investment-management
services in relation to crypto assets — nothing more. Nothing in this lane changes that.

## 7. Standing instructions for agents

1. Every regulatory statement cites a section, paragraph, or URL with a retrieval date.
2. Every draft instrument is labelled **DRAFT** with its comment-close date.
3. Rows that cannot be sourced are marked `UNSOURCED` — never filled in from plausibility.
4. "No material change" and "not published" are acceptable answers. Padding is not.
5. Flag immediately, and out of band, anything that would make a currently-allowed route
   impermissible. Do not wait for the end of a report.
6. Never describe a consultation paper as final law, however authoritative the domain.
